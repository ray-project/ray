# Experiment: C3 (second half) — does the ledger disturb anyone else?

**Pre-registered:** 2026-09-10, iteration 21, before the rig was written.

C3's first half is proven: the usable band is mapped, and at 10 % of the
262 writes/s same-key ceiling with redo capped at 1 % of `N`, 18 of 24
(rate, `N`) points admit a workable `W`. The second half has been open since
iteration 7 — *"no measurable regression in GCS p99 for other components"* — and
it is the half that decides whether the ledger design is a good citizen in a shared cluster.

## Claim

- **C3 (second half).** At the design write rate the ledger causes no measurable
  regression in GCS p99 for other components.
  *Refutes if:* at the design write rate, KV-handler latency or GCS health-check
  margin degrades measurably.

## The problem with "no measurable regression"

It is unfalsifiable as stated. A rig that cannot detect *any* regression will
report "none" and look like a pass. Two controls fix that, and they are the
whole design of this experiment:

- **Positive control (`overload`).** The same ledger driven flat out, with no
  pacing. It **must** produce a clearly measurable regression. If it does not,
  the rig cannot see regressions at all and the run is **VOID** — the `design`
  arm's clean result would mean nothing.
- **Noise floor (`idle_a` vs `idle_b`).** Two *identical* baseline arms, no
  ledger at all. Their difference is the run-to-run variation. Any effect in the
  `design` arm smaller than the `idle_a`/`idle_b` gap is indistinguishable from
  noise and must not be reported as an effect — in either direction.

Without the noise floor, "design p99 is 8 % higher than baseline" is
uninterpretable. With it, that number either exceeds the floor or it does not.

## Setup

Head + worker on rocksdb. Three independent processes:

1. **Victim** — a driver doing `internal_kv` put/get in its **own namespace**,
   timing every operation with `perf_counter`, for 60 s. This stands in for
   "another component": Serve's KV store, the Jobs API, and the dashboard all
   use exactly this path.
2. **Load** — a detached actor writing ledger keys, paced to a target rate.
3. **Harness** — starts them, collects, computes percentiles.

A second victim signal is recorded alongside: **actor-task round-trip latency**,
which exercises GCS scheduling rather than the KV handler. A KV-only regression
and a whole-GCS regression are different findings and should not be conflated.

| arm | ledger load |
|---|---|
| `idle_a` | none |
| `idle_b` | none — identical repeat, defines the noise floor |
| `design` | paced to ~26 writes/s (10 % of the 262/s same-key ceiling) |
| `overload` | unpaced, as fast as the writer can go — **positive control** |

## Thresholds (frozen before running)

- **Noise floor** `= |p99(idle_a) − p99(idle_b)| / mean(p99 idle)`.
- **Regression** = a p99 increase over baseline that exceeds **both** 25 % and
  the noise floor.
- `design` must be **below** that threshold; `overload` must be **above** it.

## Prediction (frozen)

1. **`design` shows no regression.** Confidence 0.7. 26 writes/s is ~10 % of the
   same-key ceiling and the ledger's keys are in a different namespace, so
   contention should be at the RocksDB and handler level only.
2. **`overload` shows a clear p99 regression** — at least 2×. Confidence 0.65.
   This is the clause that makes the run interpretable; if it fails the run is
   void regardless of what `design` did.
3. **Noise floor below 25 %.** Confidence 0.5, and genuinely uncertain: p99 over
   a 60 s window on a shared machine is not a quiet statistic. If the floor comes
   out *above* 25 %, then this rig cannot resolve the effect size the claim cares
   about, and the honest verdict is `uncertain` with the resolution limit stated
   — not a pass.
4. Actor round-trip p99 moves less than KV p99 in `overload`, since the ledger
   contends with the KV handler specifically. Confidence 0.55.

## What each outcome means

| `overload` | noise floor | `design` | reading |
|---|---|---|---|
| regresses | < 25 % | clean | **C3 proven.** The ledger is a good citizen at design rate, and the rig can prove it because it caught the overload. |
| regresses | < 25 % | regresses | **C3 refuted.** The design rate is already too much for a shared GCS. |
| no regression | any | any | **VOID** — blind rig. |
| regresses | ≥ 25 % | clean | `uncertain`, with the resolution limit recorded. Not a pass. |

## Amendment (after the void run, before the re-run)

`runs/…-C3interference` was **void**: `overload` at 2069 writes/s moved victim
p99 by +20 % against a **43 % noise floor**. Two identical baselines differed by
more than any treatment did.

Three signs the *measurement* was at fault, not the system:

1. The noise floor exceeded the effect size the claim cares about.
2. `overload` produced a **lower** p95 (2.46 ms vs 4.09 ms) and a **lower** actor
   round-trip p99 (12.96 ms vs 22.01 ms). Load making things faster is not a
   physical result — it means warm-up and drift across sequentially-run arms
   dominated.
3. The victim slept 10 ms between operations, issuing only ~80 ops/s. It was
   measuring wake-up jitter, not GCS.

### Redesign

- **Victim runs back-to-back**, no sleep — thousands of samples per arm.
- **Baselines interleaved** between every load arm; each treatment is compared
  against its *temporal neighbour*, so drift cancels. The noise floor becomes the
  spread across seven baselines rather than a single pair.
- **Load swept**: 26 → 200 → 1000 → flat-out ×1 → flat-out ×8. The point is no
  longer pass/fail at one rate but *where regression appears*, which calibrates
  the rig and maps the headroom at the same time.
- **An independent positive control (`bigvalue`)**: four loaders writing **4 MB**
  values flat out. This does not depend on the ledger at all. If the victim
  metric cannot detect *that*, it cannot detect anything, and the run is void.

### Added prediction

5. **`bigvalue` regresses clearly** — this is now the void condition, replacing
   the old `overload` arm which is too weak to serve as a control.
   Confidence 0.8.
6. **No ledger-shaped load regresses, up to flat-out ×8.** Confidence 0.55. The
   hypothesis carried out of the void run is that `internal_kv` is simply not a
   contended resource at any rate the ledger design can generate. If `flat_8` *does* regress,
   the rate at which it starts is the number the design needs, and it should be
   compared against the 26/s design rate to state the margin explicitly.

## Amendment 2 (after the redesigned run, before the resolution run)

`runs/…-C3interference2` worked as a rig: the independent positive control
(`bigvalue`) fired at **+1794 %** (p99 66.5 ms vs 3.5 ms), so the victim metric
demonstrably detects regressions. Results:

| arm | load | p99 | vs neighbour baseline |
|---|---|---|---|
| `r26` *(design)* | 26/s | 3.81 ms | +19.1 % |
| `r200` | 200/s | 4.91 ms | +83.0 % |
| `r1000` | 1000/s | 5.02 ms | +47.9 % |
| `flat_1` | 1840/s | 5.47 ms | +55.0 % |
| `flat_8` | 5712/s | 11.73 ms | +301 % |
| `bigvalue` *(pos ctrl)* | 60/s × 4 MB | 66.51 ms | +1794 % |

The seven interleaved baselines spread 2.68–3.53 ms, a **26.2 %** noise floor —
just above the pre-registered 25 % resolution requirement. By the rule frozen in
the original card that verdict is **`uncertain`, with the resolution limit
recorded — not a pass**, even though the design arm came in below threshold.
Recording it that way.

Two things are nonetheless established and worth keeping:

- The rig is **not blind** — `bigvalue` proves it.
- Regression appears somewhere between **26/s and 200/s**, so the design rate has
  roughly an order of magnitude of headroom. `r200` showing a larger delta than
  `r1000` is baseline noise (`base_1` was the lowest of the seven), not a
  non-monotonicity worth explaining.

### Resolution run

The only honest way to convert `uncertain` into a verdict is to make the
instrument finer, and to say how in advance:

- **60 s arms** instead of 30 s — roughly 50 k samples per arm, so p99 rests on
  ~500 tail observations instead of ~250.
- **Nine interleaved baselines**, and the threshold becomes **2σ of the baseline
  p99 distribution** rather than max−min spread, which is dominated by its two
  most extreme members.
- **The 26 → 200 range is swept** (26, 50, 100, 200), since "where does it start"
  is the number the design actually needs.
- **p95 is reported alongside p99.** In the previous run p95 was strikingly
  stable across baselines (1.132 / 1.134 ms), so it may resolve far smaller
  effects. It is declared here as a *secondary, pre-registered* signal — not
  substituted for p99 after the fact.

### Added prediction

7. **Baseline 2σ/mean falls below 25 %** with the longer arms. Confidence 0.6.
8. **`r26` stays below threshold on both p95 and p99.** Confidence 0.7.
9. **The first arm to regress is `r100` or `r200`**, giving the design rate 4–8×
   headroom in rate. Confidence 0.5.
