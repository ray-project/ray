# Experiment: C26 — does an async ledger writer buy liveness, and at what price?

**Pre-registered:** 2026-09-10, iteration 16, before the writer was implemented.

C7 was refuted because `commit()` is a **synchronous `internal_kv` put on the
unit loop's critical path**: a GCS outage froze the coordinator at unit 48 for
the full 35 s. That is not a defect so much as a fork, and C26 is the other
branch — move the commit off the critical path behind a **bounded queue**.

## Claim

- **C26.** An async ledger writer with a bounded queue lets work continue through
  a GCS outage, at the cost of a redo window bounded by **queue depth** rather
  than by `W`.
  *Refutes if:* it does not increase completed work during an outage, **or** it
  widens the redo window beyond the queue bound, **or** it reorders commits so
  the ledger is no longer contiguous.

Three clauses, and — per the rule this loop adopted after C1→C16 and C2→C22 —
each one is separately measured below. A clause that is not in the refutation
condition is not being tested.

## The prediction that matters is not "it helps"

Of course it helps. The question is **by how much, and bounded by what**, and
the honest answer is that the liveness bought is *finite and computable*:

> work continues for about `Q` more units, and then stops anyway.

At `unit_seconds=0.35` the loop produces ~2.8 units/s, so a 35 s outage demands
~98 units of buffer. With `Q=64` the queue must **fill and then block**. That is
deliberate: an arm that sailed through the outage would prove only that the
queue was bigger than the test, which is not a property of the design.

## Arms

| arm | queue | outage | measures |
|---|---|---|---|
| `async_outage` | `Q=64` | yes, in place | clause 1 — work during outage |
| `async_nokill` | `Q=64` | no | **negative control** — async must not corrupt |
| `async_crash` | `Q=64` | coordinator killed mid-flight | clause 2 — redo window |
| `sync_crash` | `Q=0` | coordinator killed mid-flight | baseline for the redo window |

The sync/outage baseline is `runs/…-C7inplace2` and is not repeated.

## Prediction (frozen)

1. **Clause 1 — work during the outage.** `async_outage` completes strictly more
   units during the outage than the sync baseline's **zero**, and the excess is
   `≈ Q`, after which it blocks on a full queue. Confidence 0.8. Predicted
   band: 50–75 units. A result far above `Q + W` would mean the queue is not
   bounding anything and I have measured the wrong thing.
2. **Clause 2 — the redo window.** On a mid-flight kill,
   `redo = executed_before_crash − resumed_from` must satisfy
   `redo ≤ Q + W` for `async_crash`, and `redo ≤ W` for `sync_crash`.
   Confidence 0.7. This is the price, and it must be *paid in the amount
   predicted*, not merely "somewhat larger".
3. **Clause 3 — contiguity.** The recovered `done` set is contiguous with no
   duplicates in **every** arm. Confidence 0.85. A single writer thread draining
   a FIFO should preserve order; if it does not, the design is unsafe and C26 is
   refuted outright regardless of the other two clauses.
4. **`async_nokill` matches sync exactly** — 400 units, contiguous, no dupes.

## Negative controls

- **NC-nokill.** If the async control corrupts or loses commits with no fault at
  all, the writer is broken and every other arm is meaningless → **VOID**.
- **NC-queue-bound.** `async_outage` must be observed to *block*: queue depth
  must reach `Q` and progress must then flatten. If work continues indefinitely,
  the queue is not bounded, backpressure is not working, and the "bounded redo
  window" claim has no basis → the run cannot prove clause 2.
- **NC-sync-baseline.** `sync_crash` must show `redo ≤ W`. If sync also shows a
  large redo window, the metric is measuring something other than the queue.

## What each outcome means

| clause 1 | clause 2 | reading |
|---|---|---|
| more work, ≈`Q` | `redo ≤ Q+W` | **C26 proven.** A real, priced fork: liveness for redo. The REP should offer both and default to sync. |
| more work, unbounded | any | queue not bounding; rig wrong, or backpressure missing |
| more work | `redo > Q+W` | **C26 refuted** — the price is not the advertised one, which is worse than not offering the option |
| no more work | — | **C26 refuted**; the outage blocks something other than the put |

## Why this may not be worth adopting even if proven

Worth stating up front, so a green result is not read as a recommendation. The
sync design's failure mode under outage is *the job pauses and then continues* —
no lost work, no redo, no correctness question. The async design converts that
into *the job keeps running and then redoes up to `Q+W` units*. For long jobs
with expensive units, pausing is plainly better. C26 earns its place only where
units are cheap and the completion rate is high — which is precisely the regime
stage B found the ledger design is worst in.
