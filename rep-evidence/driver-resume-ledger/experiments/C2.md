# Experiment: C2 (+ C3's testable half)

**Pre-registered:** 2026-09-10, before `poc/costmodel.py` was written.

## Claims

**C2.** With coalescing window `W`, a job of `N` units costs `≤ ceil(N/W) + O(1)`
durable writes, a redo window `≤ W + in-flight`, and a steady-state key count
independent of `N`.

*Refutes if:* recovery requires reading `O(N)` keys, or key count grows with `N`
under sustained operation.

**C3 (testable half).** At the target job shape the ledger write rate stays
below 10% of the measured GCS durable-write ceiling. The other half — *no
measurable regression in GCS p99 for other components* — needs a real cluster
and belongs to stage C.

## Setup

`poc/costmodel.py`, reusing `protocol.py` unmodified and counting the fsyncs it
actually issues rather than a hand-derived formula. Fsyncs are the currency
because `WriteOptions::sync = true` unconditionally
(`rocksdb_store_client.cc:104-117`), so one mutation is one fsync at ~3.81 ms.

- `N ∈ {10, 100, 1000, 10000}`, `W ∈ {1, 10, 100, 1000}`, `K ∈ {2, 4, 8, 16}`
- Recovery cost measured as the number of KV operations a fresh coordinator
  issues during carry-forward
- Ceilings from REP-64: **260 writes/s** same-key (strand-serialised) and
  **593 ops/s** aggregate with group commit
- Completion-rate sweep from 0.1 to 10⁵ units/s — deliberately far past the
  ceiling, because the fine-grained end is where the ledger design must break and that is the
  result worth reporting

## Prediction (frozen)

1. **C2's write-count clause is refuted.** `ceil(N/W) + O(1)` ignores
   compaction. Each compaction costs 1 base put + 1 pointer put + up to `K`
   segment deletes + 1 old-base delete, and there are `ceil(N/W)/K` of them, so
   the true cost should be

   ```
   writes ≈ ceil(N/W) · (1 + (K+3)/K) = ceil(N/W) · (2 + 3/K)
   ```

   — about **2.4×** the claimed figure at `K = 8`, and **3.5×** at `K = 2`. A
   constant factor, not an additive `O(1)`. I expect the measured ratio to land
   within ±10% of that formula; a larger gap means I have mis-modelled
   compaction rather than that the claim is fine.

2. **C2's key-count clause holds.** Steady-state keys `≈ K + 2` and flat in `N`
   across three orders of magnitude. Iteration 1 already saw 3 keys for
   `N ∈ {6, 8, 12}`.

3. **C2's recovery clause holds.** Carry-forward issues `O(K)` operations, not
   `O(N)`.

4. **C2's redo clause holds** — already evidenced: `INV-REDO` (redo ≤
   crashes × W) never fired in ~16k traces in iteration 1.

5. **C3's testable half holds only inside a band, and the band is narrower than
   the design doc's arithmetic suggests**, because of (1). The doc computes
   `writes/sec = completion_rate / W` and concludes a 50k-shard job at 20
   shards/s with `W = 200` sits at 0.04% of the ceiling. With the ~2.4× factor
   that becomes ~0.1% — still comfortable, so the doc's *conclusion* survives
   even though its *formula* does not. I expect the 10%-of-ceiling limit to bite
   at a completion rate around **`W · 26` units/s** rather than `W · 60`.

## Thresholds

- **C2 REFUTED** if the measured write count exceeds `ceil(N/W) + c` for any
  constant `c` — i.e. if the overhead is multiplicative. **PROVEN** only if the
  overhead is genuinely additive.
- **C2's other three clauses** reported separately, since a claim with four
  clauses that fails one is not simply "false".
- **C3-half PROVEN** if a usable band exists: some `(W, rate)` region where
  writes stay under 10% of 260/s *and* expected redo stays under 1% of `N`.
- **REFUTED** if no such region exists for any realistic job shape.

## Negative control

The cost model is arithmetic over measured counters, so the meaningful control
is a **consistency check**: `fsyncs` reported by `kvfake` must equal the number
of `Put`/`Delete` operations the protocol issued, counted independently by the
harness. If those two disagree the model is measuring something other than what
it claims, and the run is void.

## Result

- Run id: `runs/20260910-045229-C2`. An earlier attempt crashed on an output
  formatting bug before writing any results and was removed; it produced no
  evidence to preserve.
- Outcome: **C2 proven · C22 (split out) refuted · C3-half proven**

### The claim was broader than its refutation condition

C2's *statement* has four clauses; its `refutes_if` names only two — O(N)
recovery reads, and key count growing with N. Neither happened. But the
write-count clause **is** false, and it is not covered by the refutation
condition, so it cannot be used to refute C2.

Rather than quietly widen the refutation condition after seeing the result, the
false clause is split out as **C22** and marked refuted on its own terms. This
is the second time in the loop that a claim has been broader than the thing that
could kill it — C16 came out of C1 the same way. That is a claim-hygiene defect
worth naming: **every clause needs to be in the refutation condition, or it is
not being tested.**

### Measured, per clause

| clause | outcome |
|---|---|
| writes ≤ `ceil(N/W) + O(1)` | **FALSE** → C22. Overhead is *multiplicative*: mean **×2.79**, range ×2.17–×3.80 over 36 compacting configurations |
| redo ≤ `W` + in-flight | holds (iteration 1: `INV-REDO` never fired in ~16k traces) |
| steady-state key count independent of `N` | **holds.** `K=8, W=1`: **12 keys** at N = 10, 100, 1,000 and 10,000 alike. Saturates at ≈ `K+4` once `N/W ≥ K` |
| recovery reads `O(K)` not `O(N)` | **holds.** Max **33** KV operations at any N up to 10⁴ |

The frozen prediction was `2 + 3/K`. Maximum relative error against it: **8.6%**
across the 36 compacting points — so the mechanism was correctly modelled, and
the design doc's `writes/sec = completion_rate / W` understates cost by ~2.8×.
Its *conclusions* survive because they had orders of magnitude of headroom, but
the formula is wrong and every band boundary moves in by 2.8×.

### Surprise

Two, both about regimes rather than magnitudes.

1. **A non-compacting regime exists and behaves completely differently.** When
   `ceil(N/W) < K` the job finishes before compaction ever triggers, and the
   overhead ratio collapses to 1.3–4.0 driven entirely by the fixed startup cost
   rather than by compaction. The first analysis lumped these in and reported a
   40% error against the formula; separating them dropped it to 8.6%. The
   formula was never wrong — it was being tested outside its domain.
2. **Key count is flat in N but only after a ramp.** `K=16, W=10` gives 7 keys at
   N=10, 16 at N=100, then **20 at both N=1,000 and N=10,000**. "Independent of
   N" is a statement about steady state, and the steady state takes `K` segments
   to reach.

### The band, quantified

Budget: 10% of the 262 writes/s same-key ceiling, redo ≤ 1% of N, measured ×2.79
overhead.

| completion rate | N=1,000 | N=50,000 | N=10⁶ |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.1–1 /s | W = 1..20 | 1..1,000 | 1..10,000 |
| 10 /s | 2..20 | 2..1,000 | 2..10,000 |
| 100 /s | **W = 20 only** | 20..1,000 | 20..10,000 |
| 1,000 /s | **NONE** | 200..1,000 | 200..10,000 |
| 10,000 /s | NONE | **NONE** | 2,000..10,000 |
| 10⁵ /s | NONE | NONE | **NONE** |

This is the "band, not half-plane" the design doc asserted, now with edges. The
diagonal is the whole story: **The ledger design is viable exactly when the job is long enough
that a coarse `W` still leaves redo small.** A small job that completes quickly
has nowhere to stand — at N=1,000 and 100 units/s there is exactly one usable
window and above that, none. A million-unit job tolerates 10,000 units/s.

**Where the ledger design breaks, stated plainly:** fine-grained, high-throughput,
short-duration work. Which is precisely the shape the design doc guessed at, and
now it is measured rather than guessed.
