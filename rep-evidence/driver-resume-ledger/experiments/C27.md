# Experiment: C27 — the corrected redo bound, swept

**Pre-registered:** 2026-09-10, iteration 18, before the sweep was written.

C26 was refuted because the measured redo window was 30 units against my stated
bound of `Q·W + W = 27`. The excess was fully explained on inspection — but an
explanation invented *after* seeing the number is exactly the thing this loop
does not accept as evidence. C27 is that explanation turned back into a
prediction and tested on its own terms.

## Claim

- **C27.** With an async ledger writer the redo window after a mid-outage crash
  is **exactly `Q·W + 2W`** units: the bounded queue (`Q` batches of `W`), plus
  the batch the writer has dequeued and is blocked committing, plus the batch the
  producer has filled and is blocked enqueuing.
  *Refutes if:* measured redo differs from `Q·W + 2W` at any swept `Q`, or fails
  to scale linearly in `Q`.

This is a **point prediction, not a bound** — deliberately. "≤ some bound" is
nearly unfalsifiable at a single operating point; an exact formula across a
sweep can fail in four distinguishable ways (wrong intercept, wrong slope,
non-linear, noisy).

## Sweep

`Q ∈ {2, 4, 8, 16}` batches at `W=3`, plus `Q=0` (sync) as the control.

| `Q` | buffer (units) | predicted redo `= W(Q+2)` |
|---|---|---|
| 0 *(sync)* | 0 | **3** (`= W`) |
| 2 | 6 | **12** |
| 4 | 12 | **18** |
| 8 | 24 | **30** |
| 16 | 48 | **54** |

The `Q=8` row is the observation that generated the hypothesis, so it proves
nothing on its own. The load-bearing rows are `Q=2`, `4` and `16`: intercept and
slope must both come out right at points that had no say in forming the formula.

## Measurement precision

The redo window is `executed_at_death − resumed_from`. Iteration 16 read
`executed_at_death` from a 2 s sampler, which at ~2.8 units/s blurs the count by
up to ~6 units — larger than the gap between adjacent sweep points. That is
useless for an exact-formula claim.

So `die()` now dumps `progress()` to a **local file** at the instant of death,
immediately before `os._exit`. Local, because this path must work with GCS down;
instantaneous, because sampling error would swamp the effect.

## Setup

Per arm: fresh cluster on rocksdb, coordinator with queue depth `Q`, `W=3`,
`K=3`, `unit_seconds=0.35`. Kill `gcs_server` in place at t=15 s; let the queue
saturate; have the coordinator kill itself at **+30 s into the outage** via its
cached handle; restore `gcs_server` at +45 s; let the actor restart and read
`resumed_from`.

45 s keeps the outage under C29's 60 s cluster-death ceiling. +30 s is enough for
even `Q=16` (48 units of buffer, ~17 s to fill) to saturate.

## Negative controls

- **NC-sync.** `Q=0` must give redo `= W = 3` and must **not** move with `Q`. If
  the sync arm scales too, the metric is tracking something other than the queue.
- **NC-saturation.** Each async arm must be observed at `queued == Q` at death.
  An arm that dies with a partly-full queue tests a smaller buffer than labelled,
  and its point is invalid — this is the failure that starved iteration 16.

## Prediction (frozen)

1. `redo = 3·(Q+2)` exactly, at every swept `Q`. Confidence 0.6.
2. Linear fit slope `= W = 3` units per batch, intercept `= 2W = 6`.
   Confidence 0.65.
3. All arms contiguous, no duplicates. Confidence 0.9.

## What each outcome means

| observation | reading |
|---|---|
| exact at all four | **C27 proven.** The redo window is a *designable* quantity: pick `Q` from the redo budget. |
| right slope, wrong intercept | the queue accounting is right and the in-flight accounting is not; refute and re-derive the constant |
| non-linear | something other than the queue dominates — refutes, and matters more |
| exact only at `Q=8` | the formula was fitted to its own generating observation; refute |
