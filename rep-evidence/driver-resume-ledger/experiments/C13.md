# Experiment: C13 (+ new claim C19)

**Pre-registered:** 2026-09-10.

> **Disclosure.** As with `C1.md`, the rig extension needed for this experiment
> (N concurrent instances, and instances sharing an id) had to be built and run
> before the card could be written, and doing so surfaced two things. Both are
> stated here as predictions *before* the official run, but neither is blind.
> The fix for C19 and the experiment that tests it **are** pre-registered blind,
> at the bottom.

## Claims under test

**C13 (existing, load-bearing).** The coordinator constructor is idempotent
against the ledger, so a transient duplicate arising from the
`RESTARTING`-at-GCS-death window cannot corrupt or advance committed progress.

*Refutes if:* any constructor-level interleaving advances the ledger or
double-commits a unit without the second instance losing its epoch claim.

**C19 (new, load-bearing).** C17's result — that the reordered carry-forward
read protects the new epoch from a fenced writer — extends to **more than two**
concurrent instances.

*Refutes if:* with three concurrent instances, a unit acked by an instance
holding the top epoch is not recoverable after a higher epoch carries forward.

## Why C13's sharp edge is an identity question

The `RESTARTING` window (`gcs_actor_manager.cc:1846-1858`) produces two
processes of the **same actor**. So the interesting question is not whether the
constructor is idempotent in the abstract — it is what the constructor uses as
its instance id in M1's read-back step:

```python
_internal_kv_put(f"{job}/epoch/{e}", instance_id, overwrite=False, namespace=NS)
return _internal_kv_get(f"{job}/epoch/{e}", namespace=NS) == instance_id
```

If `instance_id` is derived from the **actor id** — which is stable across
restarts, available from `ray.get_runtime_context()`, and by far the most
natural thing an implementer reaches for — then **both duplicates write the same
bytes and both read them back successfully.** The read-back check passes for
both. M1's entire fencing guarantee evaporates, silently, and only for the exact
failure mode M1 exists to handle.

The id must be per-**incarnation**: a fresh uuid minted inside `__init__`, never
anything Ray hands you that survives a restart.

## Setup

- Rig: `python3 poc/run_checker.py --experiment C13 --out runs/<id>`
- New: `run_interleaving(..., spawns=[...], same_id=...)` — N instances, with
  optional shared identity.
- Sweep: two instances across all spawn points (regression); three instances
  over a coarser spawn grid; windows `w ∈ {4, 5}`; fuzz seeds `[1, 2, 3]`.
- Workloads `(4,1,2)` and `(6,1,2)`.

## Prediction (frozen)

1. **C13 is proven, conditional on per-incarnation ids.** With distinct ids,
   zero `INV-ONE` even when both duplicates race inside the constructor,
   including spawn points 0–3 where both are still in `__init__`.
2. **NC14 (`same_id`) is red on `INV-ONE`.** Both duplicates read back the
   shared id and both proceed as owner. Observed in bring-up; predicted here to
   reproduce at ~30 violations on the tiny workload.
3. **C19 is refuted**, with ~290 `INV-DUR` violations on the three-instance
   sweep. Predicted mechanism, stated in full so a different one counts as a
   surprise:
   - C's carry-forward scans epochs **descending**, and that scan is itself
     non-atomic across epochs.
   - C reads epoch 2's pointer, finds nothing (B has claimed epoch 2 but has not
     yet published its base), and falls through to epoch 1.
   - B then publishes epoch 2 **and sweeps epoch 1**.
   - C's read of epoch 1 now returns nothing, because the sweep deleted it. C
     carries forward an **empty** state.
   - The C17 fix does not help: it makes deletions *within* an epoch safe by
     ensuring a base is published before segments are deleted. The **sweep** has
     no such discipline — it deletes an entire epoch unconditionally.
4. `INV-PTR` and `INV-GC` stay clean throughout.

## Thresholds

- **C13 PASS:** zero `INV-ONE` with distinct ids, **and** NC14 red.
- **C13 FAIL:** any `INV-ONE` with distinct ids.
- **C19 REFUTED:** ≥ 1 `INV-DUR` in the three-instance sweep.
- **VOID:** NC14 green (the rig cannot see the identity collision), or the
  two-instance regression shows `INV-DUR` (the C17 fix has been broken by the
  refactor rather than by the third instance).

The two-instance regression is the important control here. Without it, a
three-instance `INV-DUR` could equally be explained by the N-instance refactor
having broken something, and the whole result would be uninterpretable.

## Negative controls

| control | mutation | expected |
|---|---|---|
| **NC14** `same_id` | all instances share one id, as an actor-derived id would | `INV-ONE` RED |
| NC13 `carry_forward_ptr_first` | the pre-C17 read order | `INV-DUR` RED |
| NC2 `fence_mode=inverted` | inverted return read as "I won" | `INV-LIVE` RED |
| 2-instance regression | unmodified protocol, two instances | must be CLEAN of `INV-DUR` |

## The fix for C19 (pre-registered blind, before implementation)

The same principle that fixed C16, applied one level up.

> **Scan epochs ascending — lowest first, highest last — and take the highest
> readable state found.**

Argument, committed before writing the code: epoch `e`'s state is deleted only
by a sweep from some epoch `m > e`, and that sweep runs *after* epoch `m` has
published its own `base_ptr`. So "epoch `e` is unreadable" implies "some epoch
`m > e` was published before `e` was deleted". A reader that visits `e` before
`m` therefore visits `m` **after** `m` was published, and finds it. Descending
order gets this exactly backwards: it looks at `m` too early and at `e` too
late, and the window between those two mistakes is the bug.

Note the symmetry with the C17 fix, which is the reason to believe it: *read in
the order that makes "I missed X" imply "X's replacement is already published."*
Within an epoch that means segments before the pointer; across epochs it means
low before high. Two instances of one rule.

Iteration 5 implements it, keeps descending order as a control, and requires the
control to go red on this exact counterexample.

## Result (filled in after the run)

- Run id: `runs/20260910-043934-C13`
- Outcome: **C13 proven (conditionally) · C19 refuted**

| prediction | outcome |
|---|---|
| (1) C13 proven with per-incarnation ids | **held.** Zero `INV-ONE` across 4,950 two-instance and 3,000 three-instance traces, including spawn points 0–3 where both duplicates are inside `__init__` |
| (2) NC14 red on `INV-ONE`, ~30 violations | **held**, 62. `epoch_owners = {1: ['A', 'A']}` — one epoch, claimed twice, by the same identity |
| (3) C19 refuted, ~290 `INV-DUR` | **held**, and much larger: **5,448** |
| (4) `INV-PTR` / `INV-GC` clean | **held** |
| 2-instance regression clean of `INV-DUR` | **held** — 4,950 traces, zero. So the third instance is the cause, not the N-instance refactor |

### Surprise

Not the direction — both predictions landed — but the **magnitude**. The
three-instance sweep produced `INV-DUR` in roughly **as many traces as it ran**
(1,146 violations in 1,110 traces at `w = 5`), against zero for two instances on
the same code. This is not a narrow race that needs a precise interleaving. Once
a third coordinator exists, losing the ledger is close to the *default* outcome.

Mechanically that follows from the sweep being unconditional: with two
instances, whoever sweeps is the only reader; with three, the sweeper and the
reader are different processes, and the reader is looking at the epoch being
swept. The two-instance case was not merely a smaller version of the
three-instance case — it structurally could not exhibit the bug. **A rig that
only ever modelled two instances would have shipped this.**

Second, smaller: NC13 (the pre-C17 read order) came back with no `INV-DUR` in
*this* run's narrower sweep (`max_spawn=30`, `w=5`) though it was firmly red in
`runs/20260910-043110-C16`. Recorded rather than reinterpreted: it means NC13 is
a weak control at this sweep size, not that the C17 result has changed.

### Envelope

C13 holds — **conditional on the instance id being per-incarnation**. That
condition is the whole result, and it is an implementation note sharp enough to
belong in the design doc rather than a footnote: `get_actor_id()` is stable,
convenient, and silently fatal here.

C17 is now scoped to two concurrent instances. Whether three is enough to expose
every cross-epoch class is fidelity gap **F7**, and it is unvalidated.

### Ripples

| claim | change | why |
|---|---|---|
| C13 | → **proven**, conditionally | with the identity condition promoted to a design-level requirement |
| C19 | → **refuted** | 5,448 counterexamples |
| C17 | scope narrowed | proven for two instances; the third breaks it by a different mechanism |
