# Experiment: F6 / F7 — attacking the rig's own registered gaps

**Pre-registered:** 2026-09-10, before any of these configurations were run.

Not a claim experiment. A **gap** experiment: every proof in this workspace
rests on two unvalidated assertions in `claims.json`, and until they are
attacked the proofs are conditional on them.

| gap | assertion | status |
|---|---|---|
| **F6** | a stale writer capped at 8 operations after being fenced only repeats conflict classes a longer tail would | **unvalidated** |
| **F7** | 3 concurrent incarnations expose every cross-epoch class; a 4th adds depth, not a new class | narrowed in iteration 5 (4 behaved like 3), **not closed** |

Both have already been wrong once in spirit: iteration 4 showed that *two*
instances structurally could not exhibit the C19 bug, so "N is enough" reasoning
in this design has a track record of being false. That is the reason to spend an
iteration here rather than on a new claim.

## Setup

Three sweeps, all against the **current** (post-C17, post-C20) protocol:

1. **F6:** stale tail ∈ {8, 16, 32, 64} at 2 and 3 incarnations.
2. **F7:** 5 and 6 concurrent incarnations, windows 4–5.
3. **Depth:** the sequential C6 crash enumeration at fault budget **3**
   (previously exhaustive only to 2).

Plus the standing controls, because a sweep that finds nothing is only
meaningful if the rig can still see failure at these settings: NC15 (descending
epoch scan) and NC13 (pointer-first read) must go red at **every** configuration
tested, not just at the ones where the bugs were originally found.

## Prediction (frozen)

1. **F6: no new violations at any tail length.** Confidence 0.8. Mechanism: once
   fenced, a writer's only dangerous acts are compaction (deletes within its
   epoch) and the sweep (deletes of lower epochs). Both are covered by the C17
   and C20 orderings regardless of how many times they repeat. A longer tail
   should therefore add *repetition*, not new classes — which is exactly what
   F6 asserts, and exactly what I would say right before being wrong again.
2. **F7: no new violations at 5–6 incarnations.** Confidence 0.7, lower than F6
   because the C19 bug *did* require a specific instance count, so instance
   count has demonstrated it can be a real dimension rather than a depth knob.
3. **Depth: budget 3 stays clean**, at a substantially larger trace count.
4. **Controls red everywhere.** If NC15 goes green at, say, 6 instances, that
   tells me the rig stops working at scale and the sweep proves nothing.

## Thresholds

- **GAPS NARROWED:** zero `INV-DUR` / `INV-ONE` / `INV-PTR` / `INV-GC`
  everywhere, with controls red at every configuration.
- **C17/C20 NARROWED (bad):** any violation → the proofs are scoped to the
  configurations already tested and the gap is promoted to a refutation.
- **VOID:** a control goes green at a configuration where the real protocol was
  also clean — the rig has stopped seeing failure and neither result counts.

## Result

- Run id: `runs/20260910-054022-GAPS`. **`runs/20260910-053138-GAPS` is retained
  as VOID** — see below.
- Outcome: **gaps narrowed.** Predictions 1–4 all held.

| sweep | result |
|---|---|
| F6: stale tail 8 → 64, at 2 and 3 incarnations | **clean at every length** |
| F7: 5 and 6 concurrent incarnations | **clean** |
| depth: sequential crash budget 3 | **clean, 237,840 traces** |
| controls | NC15 and NC13 **red at every configuration** |

### The rig went green for the wrong reason again — twice in one iteration

**First**, the F6 sweep was measuring nothing. Trace counts were *byte-identical*
across tail lengths 8, 16, 32 and 64 — because the fenced instance always
finished before the cap bound. A sweep over an inert parameter. Caught only
because the numbers were suspiciously equal, not because anything failed.

Fixed by instrumenting the cap itself: `max_tail_actually_used` and
`traces_hitting_the_cap` are now recorded, and the harness **voids the run** if
the cap never binds in any configuration. The corrected sweep shows the cap
biting (`hit-cap=233` at tail 8) and then genuinely relaxing (`hit-cap=0` at
tail 64, with a maximum tail of 38 actually used) — which is the shape that
makes "longer tails add nothing" a measurement rather than an assumption.

**Second**, the corrected run then **voided itself**: NC13 came back GREEN at
`n=3,tail=64` and `n=5,tail=8`. The pre-registered rule says a green control at
a configuration where the real protocol is also clean voids the run, and it was
honoured.

Diagnosis, before changing anything: **the rig was not blind.** NC15 was red at
both of those exact configurations, so `INV-DUR` was observable there. NC13 was
*starved* — its sweep was too small for its bug to surface. The final run gives
both controls the sweep size at which each is known to fire, and the asymmetry
is now visible in the numbers: at the same settings **NC15 produces ~25,000
violations and NC13 produces ~100**. NC13's bug needs a much deeper interleaving,
which is exactly why a small sweep misses it.

That is a real lesson about controls, not just about this run: **a control has
its own sensitivity, and running every control at one sweep size assumes they
are equally sensitive.** They are not.

### Standing

This is the **fifth and sixth** time in this loop that a clean result had a
cause other than the design being correct. The running list:

1. controls that only tested the invariant checker, not the fault injectors
2. a partial-order reduction that starved the second instance
3. a missing `INV-LIVE`, making total livelock indistinguishable from success
4. a concurrency degree too low to contain the failure class
5. an inert stale-tail parameter
6. a control starved by too small a sweep

Every one was caught by a number looking wrong. None was caught by a failure.
