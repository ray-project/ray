# Experiment: C4 (head half) + C7 + C21 (head-restart half)

**Pre-registered:** 2026-09-10, before the multi-process harness was written.

Three claims have been waiting on one piece of infrastructure: a cluster whose
**head can be killed independently of the coordinator**. A single-node
in-process `ray.init` cannot do it — shutting the head down takes the
coordinator with it. `ray start --head` plus a second `ray start --address`
node can.

## Claims

- **C4 (head half).** A detached, named, **non-head-pinned** coordinator
  survives head loss and is reattachable by name afterwards.
  *Refutes if:* after the head restart the actor is dead, duplicated, or the
  name lookup fails.
- **C7.** GCS unavailability degrades to *"work continues, durability pauses"*
  without deadlocking the coordinator.
  *Refutes if:* the failover window deadlocks the coordinator or grows a queue
  without bound.
- **C21 (head-restart half).** `session_name` survives a real head restart —
  the half `runs/20260910-045910-stageC` could not reach.

## Setup

1. `ray start --head` with `gcs_storage=rocksdb` on a PV-like path.
2. `ray start --address=<head>` — a second raylet, so work can live off-head.
3. Create the coordinator pinned to the **worker** node
   (`NodeAffinitySchedulingStrategy`), `lifetime="detached"`,
   `max_restarts=-1`.
4. Let it commit progress. Then **`kill` the head's GCS process** and observe
   the coordinator during the outage.
5. `ray start --head` again on the **same storage path and port**.
6. Reattach from a fresh driver; compare `session_name`, epoch, ledger.

## Prediction (frozen)

1. **C4 head half: the coordinator's *process* survives the outage**, because
   it is on another node and nothing kills it. Confidence 0.6. The risk is not
   the process — it is the **actor registry**, which lives in GCS memory
   (fidelity gap F3, never modelled). On restart GCS reloads actors from its
   tables; whether a worker-node detached actor is re-adopted rather than
   re-created is exactly what `gcs_actor_manager.cc:1846-1858` governs and what
   this test finally exercises.
2. **The most likely failure is the ~20 s node health-check budget**
   (`ray_config_def.h:1072-1078`: 5,000 ms + 5 × 3,000 ms). If the head is down
   longer than that, the new GCS may mark the worker node dead, `OnNodeDead`
   fires, and the coordinator is **restarted rather than re-adopted**. That
   would not refute C4 — the ledger makes a restart survivable — but it turns
   "survives head loss" into "is restarted after head loss", which is a weaker
   and honest claim.
3. **C7: work continues, durability pauses.** The coordinator should keep
   executing units and block on its ledger writes. Confidence 0.5 — the real
   risk is ray#55996: `DEADLINE_EXCEEDED` is *not* in the retried set
   (`grpc_util.h:127-133`), so a timed-out `InternalKVPut` can wedge the caller
   permanently.
4. **C21: `session_name` unchanged**, since it is read from the storage path.
   Confidence 0.9.
5. **Ledger intact and resume correct** after the restart.

## Thresholds

- **C4 head half PROVEN** if a fresh driver can reattach by name afterwards and
  the ledger's committed progress is intact — whether the coordinator was
  re-adopted or restarted. Recorded separately, because the distinction matters
  to the design even if the claim survives either way.
- **REFUTED** if the actor is gone, duplicated, or the ledger lost progress.
- **C7 PROVEN** if the coordinator makes progress during the outage and is not
  wedged after it.
- **C7 REFUTED** if it deadlocks or is still stuck after the head returns.
- **INCONCLUSIVE** if the harness cannot bring the head back on the same
  address — a harness limit, not a result.
